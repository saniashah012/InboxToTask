# Full Citation & Link: Complete APA reference with active URL.

Bellotti, V., Ducheneaut, N., Howard, M., & Smith, I. (2003). Taking email to task: The design and evaluation of a task management centered email tool. Proceedings of the SIGCHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, 345–352. https://doi.org/10.1145/642611.642672

# Structured Summary (4–6 sentences): Research Problem, Methodology, and Main Findings.
This essay is mainly about how people use emails to manage tasks and why traditional emails are hard for users to achieve this purpose. The researchers recruit 11 participants and analyze their email. They also interview these participants to check their findings. They develop a Threaded Task-Centric Collections based on their findings.

# Three Key Insights (3): 
1. There is the lack of integration between email, task list, outbox, and calendar in email system. However, a simple task often contains those four different types of innformation. 

2. It is the volume of email and to-dos for oneself that cause trouble to email users, not email structure.

3. What matters the most to user's sense of overload are how many email threads the user needs to follow everyday and the time interval between messages in the same thread.

# Two Limitations or Risks (2): Weaknesses, edge cases, or ethical/technical concerns identified.
1. This experiment only has eleven partivcipants which is a small smaple size.

2. The participants' emails are recorded and analyzed. Although the participants deleted the personal emails they got, there is still risk that those participants don't have enough time to take look at every email and leave some private ones in record.

# One Concrete Inspiration (1): A specific design idea, system pattern, or feature this paper inspires for your project.
The concept of Thrasks inspired us to design InboxToTask around tasks rather than individual emails. Instead of simply extracting a deadline from an email, our system could create a task card that keeps the action, deadline, people involved, original email, and related attachments together. We could also support “waiting on” states for tasks that depend on another person’s response. This would help users maintain the context surrounding an action instead of treating each email as an isolated task.
# Email overload: Exploring personal information management of email. Proceedings of the SIGCHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems
# Full Citation & Link: Complete APA reference with active URL.
Whittaker, S., & Sidner, C. (1996). Email overload: Exploring personal information management of email. Proceedings of the SIGCHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems, 276–283. https://doi.org/10.1145/238386.238530

# Structured Summary (4–6 sentences): Research Problem, Methodology, and Main Findings.
The researchers collected quantitative mailbox data from 18 users, including:

The total number of messages in each mailbox
The age of messages
The size of messages
The number of messages in each archive folder
The number of conversation threads

The researchers originally wanted to conduct longitudinal data collection to observe how the size and structure of users’ mailboxes changed over time.

However, because repeatedly accessing users’ personal email data was difficult in practice, they were only able to collect a “snapshot” of each user’s mailbox at a particular point in time.
They also conducted structured interviews with the participants. Some findings includes: The inbox functions as a task manager and email systems should support task management directly.

# Three Key Insights (3): 
1. There was a theory called "One-touch model": all the emails should be unread or filed. However, this is not true. That's because users find it hard to file emails from inbox, since filing itself is hard and it is not beneficial enough to create folders.

2. There are four types of emails that users won't response quickly. (1) To-dos: complicated work that requires days to do. (2) To-reads: long message. (3) Ongoing correspondence. (4) Messages of indeterminate status.

3. Email needs to be redesign to support filing, task management and asynchronous communication at the same time.

# Two Limitations or Risks (2): Weaknesses, edge cases, or ethical/technical concerns identified.
1. All participants were employees of Lotus Development Corporation.

2. They didn't collect enough email data but snapshot of user's email inbox.
