## 4. Design System Alignment

Selected Design System: IBM Carbon Design System. InboxToTask is a desktop-focused task review application that will have to deal with dense structured information. Carbon contains components and reuse principles for structured data, task operations, and feedback system. All component behaviour, spacing, font, color token, focus state and accessibility design are aligned with Carbon and do not mix components styles from other design systems.

-  Task and Information List  : Use the Carbon data table or list for the row hierarchy, and fine dividers, selected and hover states, as well as sources and operation entry points. The list should contain the task title, person in charge, deadline, status and source. The Carbon data table is using layers and text tokens to distinguish table headers, list rows, selected states and focus states. [the data form specification] (https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/data-table/specifications)

-  Primary and secondary operations:  Each interface will highlight the most important operations at the moment, such as "Approve and Add". "Edit", "View Source", "Skip" and "Undo" will use secondary or tertiary buttons. The button labels must accurately describe the operation. Focus, hover, press and disabled states must be aligned with the Carbon guidelines. [] button specification (https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/button/specifications)

-  Processing progress and status feedback:  Show the progress indicators and status text while analyzing emails; Use the embedded notification for the "Review Required" status, and show the toast notification after the successful calendar update. After the notification disappears, the task line must continue showing the confirmation details. The status must not be indicated by the color alone; use the text and icons in conjunction. [notice guide] (https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/core/components/notification/guidelines)

-  Trust and AI Behavior:  Mark task fields as generated by AI, display excerpts from the original email, highlight uncertain or verifications of the fields. The decision-making power for task approval and external operations belongs to the students. Such design is aligned with the recommendations of Carbon for trusted AI experience. [Carbon for AI](https://www.carbondesignsystem.com/building-blocks/foundations/carbon-for-ai)

-  Accessibility Design  : Operations like review, editing, approval, skipping and revocation must be supported by the keyboard interface and focus state. Controls which use icons only must have descriptive labels; warnings and success statuses must be displayed through text and icons, not just the color alone.

Step 6 describes the roles and cooperation of the humans and AI from three cognitive dimensions: reasoning, memory, and attention, and explains how to achieve the coordination via the decision-making power and upgraded processing mechanism. Step 7 focuses on choosing the priority and interface elements according to the match between empirical evidence and theory. One respondent fears that the AI may misconstrue deadlines, miss requirements or attribute actions of others to the students. Another respondent underlined the importance of the verification. Memory + reasoning: AI extracts and organizes the email information; people make the judgment using the combination of the situation. Showing the information source will help people verify the AI reasoning. Priority 1 - Evidence-based task review. Show the task, person in charge, deadline, requirements and excerpt of the original email simultaneously. Students will be able to view the original email, edit the fields and approve the task. |

Both respondents mentioned the importance of the verification of the information coverage and errors. DeepSeek missed 2 out of 20 target emails. Attention + Memory: AI filters the emails, but the interface must display the omissions, uncertainties and errors of the information to the human reviewer. Priority 1 - Integrity and Uncertainty Review. Show the scanning progress, the total number of emails and the number of processed emails. Mark the uncertain fields as "requires review" and continuously show the unverified items until the problem will be solved. |

One respondent wants to have his decision-making power and is against the automatic reply of the AI. Another respondent will review the important emails manually. Meta-coordination/Role division: AI prepares suggestions, and people preserve the decision-making power for the consequences of the actions. Priority 1 - Approval Checkpoint. Tasks and calendar events cannot be added automatically. The external communication must be explicitly authorized. |

Both respondents want to view the original email. This project also differentiates the task-oriented emails and information-based emails. Shared Memory: Both humans and AI need the source of information, which can be reviewed together. Priority 2 - Separate summary views with source links. Put task-related emails and information-related emails in separate views and add the "View Original Email" link for both. The information-based emails do not automatically create tasks. |

One respondent could accept the summary that takes no more than three minutes, while the other prefers it to be done in about one minute. Attention Coordination: State information and gradual presentation of the results will help users to arrange his attention during the AI processing. Priority 2 - Streaming progress feedback. Show the processing state and gradually show the result when it will be ready. Test user's perception of the response speed via the processing targets of 60 seconds and 180 seconds. |

Respondents are afraid that the AI may make the mistakes. The verification record also shows that the system lacks the information about the person in charge of the action and the person who is followed up. Reasoning + Meta-coordination: The system needs to support the correction and handling of the differences of opinions, as well as the distinction of AI suggestions from the actions which are explicitly assigned to the users. Priority 2 - Correction and Revocation. Support the fields editing, feedback of the errors and undo after synchronization. Distinguish "waiting for the reply of the other party" and "suggesting follow-up", and show the objects and the basis of suggesting follow-up. |

One respondent was afraid of sensitive information such as grades. The other one raised no similar concerns. Objectives and Constraints/Role Division: The team needs to clearly define the information, which the AI will access, and how to protect the privacy of the rest information. Priority 3 - Privacy Control. Before connecting, specify the scope of access and provide the controls for the sensitive content and task processing. Subsequently, more respondents must be asked to confirm that these controls are correct. |

Design Principles: Clear role division and coordination of the attention of the users with the verification process. InboxToTask will allow the AI to scan and organize the emails, while the students will check the basis of actions, handle the uncertainty and authorize the further actions. At Checkpoint 3, this workflow will be compared to the two benchmark approaches: process which is performed entirely by the humans and process, which is performed entirely by the AI.
