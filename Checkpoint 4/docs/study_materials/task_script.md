## Before the participant arrives (not read aloud)

- Confirm the participant code, assigned order and inbox using the protocol's counterbalancing table. Each participant completes both a manual condition and an InboxToTask condition, with matched fictional inboxes A and B.
- Confirm the finalized time limit for each condition: **[MANUAL LIMIT]** and **[INBOXTOTASK LIMIT]**. Have a timer and anonymous data sheet ready.
- Prepare the correct fictional inbox, the live InboxToTask site, and the approved Calendar task and fallback. Check that the prior participant's changes have been put back by hand under Issue #101 (there is no reset script). ## 1. Welcome and introduction

**Read aloud:**

“Thank you for joining our study. We are evaluating two ways of working with email and organizing tasks. You will try both ways using fictional study emails. We are interested in how the process works for you, not in testing your ability. Some steps may feel easy and others may feel confusing. That is useful feedback for us.”

“Please follow the instructions for each activity. You can ask me to repeat an instruction, but I may not be able to tell you what action to take while you are completing a task.”

## 2. Consent

**Read aloud:**

“Before we begin, please read the study consent information. Participation is voluntary. You may skip a question or stop participating at any time. We will use a participant code in our study records. We will not ask you to connect your personal inbox to the application.”

“Please take your time reading the form. What questions do you have before deciding whether to participate?”

**Facilitator action:** Present the approved consent form from Issue #60 and follow its exact consent procedure. Do not proceed without consent. Keep any signed consent form in the designated private shared folder, never in the public repository. **[Confirm any additional consent language, recording permissions, and retention details against the approved form.]**

## 3. Think-aloud instruction

**Read aloud:**

“While you work, please say what you are thinking. For example, you can tell us what you are looking for, what you expect to happen, or why you choose an action. You do not need to explain everything perfectly. If you become quiet, I may remind you to keep talking.”

**Neutral reminder, if needed:** “Could you tell me what you are thinking right now?”

## 4. First condition — assigned by counterbalancing

**Facilitator sequence (not read aloud):** If InboxToTask is assigned first, complete its scored email task **and the Calendar task in Section 6** before proceeding to the second (manual) condition. If manual is assigned first, complete manual first; then perform the InboxToTask scored email task and Calendar task together as the second condition. The optional exploration in Section 7 takes place only after **both conditions and the Calendar task** are complete. Follow the final protocol if it specifies a different approved sequence.

**Facilitator action:** Use the protocol assignment. The first condition may be manual or InboxToTask; do not always present the manual condition first. Read the corresponding script below. Start and stop the timer according to the finalized protocol.

### Manual condition script (use whenever manual is assigned)

**Read aloud:**

“In this activity, you will review a set of fictional emails without using InboxToTask's AI task suggestions. Please identify the tasks that you think need attention and record them using the method provided for this activity. For each task, include what needs to be done, who is responsible, and any deadline you can find. Some emails may not contain a task.”

“Please work as you normally would within the instructions. You will have **[MANUAL LIMIT]** for this activity. I will let you know when the time is up. Please continue thinking aloud.”

**Facilitator action:** Display the assigned fictional inbox and **[CONFIRM MANUAL RECORDING METHOD: worksheet, document, or other approved interface]**. Read the finalized manual task prompt from `task_prompts.md` verbatim if it differs from this general introduction. Start the timer; record completion time, identified tasks and observable corrections. At the limit, say: “The time for this activity is over. Thank you.”

### InboxToTask condition script (use whenever InboxToTask is assigned)

**Read aloud:**

“In this activity, you will use InboxToTask to review a different set of fictional study emails and identify tasks. You can examine the suggestions, check the source emails, and make corrections where you think they are needed. Some emails may not require any task.”

“Please work through the activity using the study account and the instructions on screen. You will have **[INBOXTOTASK LIMIT]** for the scored email task. Please continue thinking aloud.”

**Facilitator action:** Open the live site and assigned study inbox. Use a new Guest or private browser window for each participant. The participant runs setup on the study account and enters the same scripted setup values as every other participant (decided Nov 17; the values are listed in #59). Check every field against the script before the participant saves, because a different value makes the app analyse existing email again. Participants do not change settings at any point in the session. Automatic checks are on for the session; after setup is saved, the first check covers the past 7 days, reuses the saved analysis of every existing email, and analyses only new email. Read the finalized InboxToTask task prompt from `task_prompts.md` verbatim. Start the timer; record time, task outcomes against the answer key, corrections and source-email checks. At the limit, say: “The time for this part is over. Thank you.”

## 7. Optional exploration — after all scored tasks

**Facilitator action:** Offer this only after both scored email conditions and the Calendar task are finished, and before the questionnaire. Allow approximately 5–8 minutes. Participation is optional and unscored. Ensure the approved consent covers this activity. Do not ask the participant to connect their personal inbox. If a participant chooses to send a fictional email from their own sending account, follow the approved consent and privacy procedure; never publish their address. The exploration email is always a new email, never a reply to an email in the study inbox, and the participant does not approve or dismiss proposed changes to tasks; either can leave records the runner cannot remove in the app (decided Nov 17). Settings stay as set up. **[To be decided: whether participants may send from their own account; a sender the scripted setup does not allow is not analysed.]**

**Read aloud:**

“The scored activities are finished. There is also an optional chance to explore the application for about five to eight minutes. You may skip this without giving a reason.”

“The app has been checking for new email automatically, when you open it and every minute while it’s open. You can see this setting in Settings.”

“Please leave the settings as they are. If you would like, you can send a short made-up email containing a task to the study inbox, as a new email rather than a reply. You may use your own sending account or a provided study sender account. We will give you the study inbox address privately. Before sending it, please tell us whether you expect it to become a task suggestion.”

“You can then explore the results, including new or unread suggestions, the source email, and editing or dismissing a suggestion.”

**Optional neutral prompts:**

- “What do you expect will happen?”
- “Did that match what you expected? Why or why not?”
- If the email does not appear: “Why do you think it didn't show up?”
- “Would you trust this with your own inbox? What would you change?”

**Facilitator action:** Automatic checks are on, so allow the documented scanning interval. After the participant's prediction and observation, explain any relevant filter reason if needed. Under the participant code, record sender **type** (not address), fictional email subject/body, prediction, processed/skipped outcome and reason, suggestion fields or no suggestion, brief reaction and result screenshot. Keep exploration outcomes separate from scored-task results. Finish logging before the account is put back for the next participant.

**Facilitator action:** Provide **[APPROVED QUESTIONNAIRE LINK OR FORM]**. ## 9. Post-session interview

**Read aloud:**

“I have a few final questions about your experience. Please answer in your own words. You may skip any question.”

**Facilitator action:** Ask the finalized questions from `interview_guide.md` in their approved order. Until that guide is finalized, the following are **proposed questions**, not a substitute for the approved guide:

1. “What felt easiest or most difficult about finding tasks in the emails?”
2. “How did the manual approach compare with InboxToTask?”
3. “When did you trust or question a task suggestion?”
4. “Did checking the original email change any of your decisions?”
5. “How clear was the Calendar activity?”
6. “What would you change to make the tool easier or safer to use?”

**Facilitator action:** Record de-identified notes and any approved quotations under the participant code.

## 10. Debrief and closing

**Read aloud:**

“That is the end of the session. Thank you for sharing your time and feedback. We used fictional emails so that everyone could complete comparable activities without sharing their personal inbox. We will use the results to understand how the two approaches support task identification and where the application could improve.”

“Do you have any final questions about the study?”

**Facilitator action:** Follow the approved debrief and any withdrawal/contact instructions from the consent form. Confirm that the study data is recorded under the participant code. Do not put identifying information or signed consent in the public repository. Put the account back by hand according to Issue #101: move tasks back with Move back, not Delete (deleting a task permanently blocks that email from being checked again), delete Calendar events the participant created, remove the exploration email with Delete from InboxToTask (in its Source email dialog) and then in the study account's Gmail, then use Settings › Connections › **Clear settings and run setup again** so the next participant starts at setup. Every task must be moved back before that: a task left active makes its thread be analysed again at the next setup. Saved records are kept. "New" and "Unread" marks live in the browser, so close the Guest or private window; the next participant gets a new one.
